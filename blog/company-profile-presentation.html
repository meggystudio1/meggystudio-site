Company Profile Presentation: What to Include

By MEGGYSTUDIONovember 18, 20268 min read

A company profile is often the first real impression a client, partner or tender committee gets of your business. It needs to answer three questions fast: who are you, what do you do, and why should I trust you?

Here's what to include, in an order that flows, plus the writing and design habits that make a profile feel premium instead of generic.

  2. Writing tips for a profile people actually read

The essential slides

1. Cover

Company name, logo, a short positioning line and the year. Use a strong photo or brand visual; this slide sets the tone.

2. About us

Two or three sentences: what you do, for whom, and since when. Add a few key facts like founding year, locations or team size.

3. Vision, mission and values

Keep each one short enough to remember. Three values with one line each beat seven values nobody reads.

4. What we do (services or products)

Group your offer into 3 to 6 clear categories with an icon or image each. Explain the benefit, not just the name of the service.

5. How we work

A simple process in 4 or 5 steps shows clients what working with you feels like. It also answers "what happens after I say yes?".

6. Numbers that matter

Years in business, projects delivered, clients served, satisfaction rate, growth. Pick 3 or 4 numbers and make them big.

7. Clients and partners

Logos of brands you've worked with (with permission). If you can't show logos, show industries or a short list of project types.

8. Case studies or portfolio

Two or three projects with the challenge, what you did, and the result. Photos or screenshots make them real.

9. Testimonials

Short, specific quotes from real clients with name and role. "They delivered two weeks early" is stronger than "Great team!".

10. Team

Leadership or key people with photos and one line each. It turns a company into people the reader can trust.

11. Locations or coverage

Offices, regions served, or a map. Useful for tenders and partners who need to know your reach.

12. Contact and call to action

Email, phone, website, social links, and one clear next step: "Book a call", "Request a quote", or "Visit our showroom".

Writing tips for a profile people actually read

  • Lead with the client's benefit. "We help retailers cut delivery times" beats "We are a leading logistics provider".
  • Cut buzzwords. "Innovative", "synergy" and "one-stop solution" say nothing. Use specifics.
  • Make titles do the work. Each slide title should be a statement, not a label.
  • Prove every claim. If you say "trusted", show the clients. If you say "fast", show the timeline.

Design rules

  • One visual style. Same fonts, colors and spacing throughout. It signals a well-run company.
  • Real photos over stock clichés. Your team, office and work beat generic handshakes.
  • White space. Crowded slides look cheap. Give each element room.
  • Office fonts. If the file will be opened on other computers, fonts like Calibri or Arial keep the layout intact.
  • Subtle motion. A Morph transition between sections adds polish without distracting. Learn how.

FAQ

How long should a company profile presentation be?

For a meeting, 12 to 20 slides is usually enough. If it's a leave-behind document people read on their own, you can go longer, but keep each slide focused on one idea.

What is the difference between a company profile and a pitch deck?

A pitch deck asks for something specific, usually investment, and argues why now. A company profile introduces who you are, what you do and why clients can trust you. It's used for sales, partnerships, tenders and onboarding.

Should a company profile include prices?

Only if your pricing is simple and public. Otherwise show packages or service tiers without numbers, and share a quote separately.
